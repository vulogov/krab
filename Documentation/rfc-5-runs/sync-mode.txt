Live corpus at n=500: 14,000 objects

1. Full manifest against the per-sync window

   a full manifest is 2 x m x 16 B (both sides name what they hold)

   link                 window     manifest    fits?     max m
   tcp over Tor   1,073,741,824      448,000      yes 33,554,432
   LoRa SF10            18,000      448,000       NO       562
   courier        68,719,476,736      448,000      yes 2,147,483,648

   A LoRa link needs shard k >= 5 for a full manifest to fit at n=500.
   RFC 2 §6: k=5 leaves a 3.12% anonymity set.

2. RBSR against the round-trip budget

   descent depth at b=16, m=14,000: 4 rounds

   link                  RTT   {d} rounds     vs TTL    verdict
   tcp over Tor           6s          24s      0.0%         ok
   LoRa SF10             20s          80s      0.0%         ok
   courier          518,400s        24.0d    171.4%         NO

   RBSR control cost: 2 x 16 x 4 x 36 = 4,608 B + 16 B per difference
   against a full manifest's 448,000 B -- 97x cheaper

   => the decision procedure, from the two tables above:

        full manifest feasible  iff  2*m*16 <= per-sync window
        RBSR feasible           iff  depth * 2 * latency << TTL
        LoRa: manifest infeasible, RBSR feasible  -> RBSR
        courier: manifest feasible, RBSR infeasible -> manifest
        TCP: both feasible -> RBSR, on bytes

3. Why Bloom filters fail asymmetrically

   A false positive means the sender believes the receiver already
   holds an object, so it is never offered -- silent loss, not delay.

     peers         p=1%       p=0.1%
         1     1.00e-02     1.00e-03
         2     1.00e-04     1.00e-06
         4     1.00e-08     1.00e-12
         8     1.00e-16     1.00e-24
        12     1.00e-24     1.00e-36

   P(object never delivered) = p^peers, so the loss concentrates
   entirely on low-degree nodes -- which SIM-0 §5 already identifies
   as the population where delivery is worst. A leaf node loses 1% of
   its mail permanently at p=1%.

4. Effective retention under capacity pressure

   ingress 31.0 MB/day at n=500

     cap MB   days held   promise 30d?
        100         3.2         BROKEN
        200         6.5         BROKEN
        300         9.7         BROKEN
        450        14.5         BROKEN
       1000        32.3           kept

   effective_retention = min(promised, cap / daily_ingress)
   A node promising 30 days on a 100 MB cap actually holds 3.2, and
   SIM-1 §4 measured the resulting re-fetch loop at +68% ingress.
